## Research overview

This research examined the quinoa commercialization network in Jauja and Huancayo, two of the main quinoa-producing areas in Peru's Junín Region. The study estimated economic performance across producers and intermediaries and identified how market relationships, competition, informality, and bargaining power influenced the distribution of economic value.

The research mapped the entire commercialization process, from agricultural production to the final consumer, identifying participants, marketing channels, costs, margins, and opportunities to improve the efficiency of the value chain.

## Fieldwork and data collection

Fieldwork surveys were conducted in March 2015 for the 2013–2014 production season:

- 190 producer surveys in Jauja and Huancayo
- 17 intermediary surveys
- Research on 19 companies in the study area
- Interviews in local markets, Lima's Santa Anita wholesale market, and with Qali Warma representatives
- Four additional cost surveys across small, medium, and large producers
- Participatory workshops to map market participants and relationships

Secondary information came from the Regional Directorate of Agriculture of Junín, MINAGRI, local agricultural agencies, and institutional sources.

## Methods

- Two-Step Cluster Analysis to classify producer groups
- Commercialization network analysis across 31 identified marketing channels
- MACTOR analysis to examine stakeholder influence and alliances
- Cost and margin analysis for production and commercialization

## Key findings

The network was centralized and traditional, with wholesalers exercising substantial influence over product flows. The domestic market was the largest destination at 42.4%, followed by the regional market at 35.0%.

Average commercialization margins were approximately 32.3% for intermediaries and 12.4% for producers. Producers faced lower access to machinery, market information, and bargaining opportunities.

## Recommendations

- Strengthen producer associations and cooperatives
- Improve coordination among value-chain participants
- Increase productivity and product quality
- Establish a regional quinoa technical working group
- Expand direct market access through institutional buyers and specialty markets
