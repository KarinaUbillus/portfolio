## RESEARCH CASE STUDY 01

### 1. Research overview

This research examined the quinoa commercialization network in the provinces of Jauja and Huancayo, two of the main quinoa-producing areas in Peru’s Junín region. The study aimed to understand how the agricultural value chain operated, estimate the economic performance of producers and intermediaries, and identify how market relationships, competition, informality and bargaining power influenced the distribution of economic value. The research addressed an important challenge in agricultural markets: although quinoa production had expanded, small and medium-sized producers continued to face difficulties accessing markets, obtaining reliable market information and negotiating favourable prices. The study investigated the entire commercialization process, from agricultural production to the final consumer, identifying the different participants, marketing channels, costs, margins and opportunities to improve the efficiency of the value chain.

### 2. Fieldwork and data collection

A significant component of the research involved primary data collection and direct engagement with agricultural market participants. Fieldwork surveys were conducted in March 2015, focusing on the 2013–2014 production season.

**Producer surveys:** Designed and conducted surveys with 190 quinoa producers in Jauja and Huancayo to collect information about production systems, cultivated land, yields, sales, storage, market destinations, access to information, technology and producer associations.

**Intermediary surveys:** Collected information from 17 quinoa intermediaries (acopiadores), examining their commercial activities, purchasing and selling practices, market relationships and perspectives.

**Business and market research:** Gathered information from 19 companies operating in the study area and conducted additional interviews in local markets, the Santa Anita wholesale market in Lima, and with representatives of the Qali Warma social food programme.

**Production cost assessments:** Conducted four additional cost surveys involving small, medium and large producers to estimate production costs across different production scales and technological conditions.

**Participatory research:** Used workshops and participatory research techniques to identify market participants, understand their relationships and map the commercialization network.

Secondary information was also collected from the Regional Directorate of Agriculture of Junín (DRAJ), the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation (MINAGRI), local agricultural agencies and other institutional and academic sources.

### 3. Data analysis and methodology

The research integrated quantitative and qualitative approaches to examine both the economic performance of the value chain and the relationships among its participants.

| Method | Research application |
| --- | --- |
| Two-Step Cluster Analysis | Classified producers into groups based on quantitative and categorical characteristics including cultivated land, quinoa production, yields, production distribution, self-consumption and seeds, storage practices and machinery use. |
| Commercialization network analysis | Mapped the relationships between producers, collectors, wholesalers, retailers, processing companies and final markets. Identified 31 commercialization channels and examined how products moved through the network and reached different destinations. |
| MACTOR Analysis | Applied the MACTOR methodology (Matrix of Alliances and Conflicts: Tactics, Objectives and Recommendations) to examine stakeholder influence, dependencies, competing interests and potential alliances within the commercialization system. |
| Cost and margin analysis | Calculated production and commercialization costs, gross and net marketing margins, producer profitability and the distribution of value among the different participants in the chain. |

### 4. Key findings and results

#### Producer characteristics and market participation

The cluster analysis identified four distinct producer groups. The study identified significant differences in production scale, technology adoption, storage practices and market participation. Small producers generally had lower access to machinery, market information and opportunities to strengthen their bargaining position. The research also identified weak producer associations and limited coordination among farmers.

#### Commercialization network and market structure

The study identified 31 commercialization channels, demonstrating the complexity of the quinoa marketing system.The network was predominantly traditional and centralized, with wholesalers playing an influential role in concentrating product flows. Relationships between market participants were frequently asymmetric, competitive and informal, limiting coordination and the ability of producers to capture additional value. The domestic market represented the largest destination for quinoa production (42.4%), followed by the regional market (35.0%).

#### Distribution of economic value

The analysis of marketing margins revealed substantial differences in the economic benefits received by producers and intermediaries. Average commercialization margins obtained by intermediaries were approximately 32.3%, compared with 12.4% for producers. These differences were associated with the limited value-added activities undertaken by farmers, their weaker bargaining power and the greater control intermediaries exercised over commercialization and market access.

#### Stakeholder influence and governance

MACTOR analysis identified consumers, financial institutions and companies as the stakeholders with the greatest influence over production volumes, prices, product transformation and quality. Producers, agricultural supply stores and milling companies had comparatively less influence, while intermediaries occupied an intermediate position.

### 5. Conclusions and recommendations

The research concluded that the quinoa commercialization network in Jauja and Huancayo was complex, centralized and characterized by unequal relationships among market participants. These conditions contributed to technical, economic and innovative inefficiencies.

The study proposed several measures to strengthen the agricultural value chain:

- **Strengthen producer associations and cooperatives:** Encourage meaningful collaboration among farmers to improve bargaining power, access to information, technology and market opportunities.
- **Improve coordination among value-chain participants:** Promote more balanced and sustainable commercial relationships between producers, intermediaries, processing companies and buyers.
- **Increase productivity and product quality:** Encourage appropriate machinery use, improved crop management and organic production practices to strengthen product differentiation and competitiveness.
- **Promote institutional coordination:** Establish a regional quinoa technical working group to facilitate cooperation, knowledge exchange and coordinated action among agricultural stakeholders.
- **Expand direct market access:** Encourage direct purchasing arrangements between producers and institutional buyers, including social food programmes such as Qali Warma, as well as participation in ecological and specialty-product markets.
