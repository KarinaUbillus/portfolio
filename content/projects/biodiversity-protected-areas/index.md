## RESEARCH PROJECT 2

### 1. Research overview

This research investigated the influence of biodiversity on people’s decisions to visit protected natural areas (PNAs) in Peru and examined how environmental attributes contribute to the recreational economic value of these areas. Traditional travel cost studies generally estimate the recreational value of natural areas based on visitors’ travel expenses. However, they do not necessarily isolate the contribution of biodiversity as an individual site attribute. The research addressed this gap by explicitly incorporating biodiversity, measured through the number of flora and fauna species, into an economic model of visitors’ site-selection decisions. The central research question was whether biodiversity influenced visitors’ decisions as significantly as the total cost of travelling to a protected area. The findings aimed to provide economic evidence that could support biodiversity conservation policies, investment decisions and the sustainable management of protected natural areas.

### 2. Research design, fieldwork and data collection

The research focused on six protected natural areas selected to represent different ecological regions, environmental characteristics, accessibility conditions and tourism settings in Peru. The selection process considered the availability of biodiversity inventories and visitor statistics, the number of visits, ecological representation and differences in the characteristics of the sites.

- Paracas National Reserve.
- Manu National Park.
- Huascarán National Park.
- Pacaya Samiria National Reserve.
- Nor Yauyos Cochas Landscape Reserve.
- Tumbes Mangroves National Sanctuary.

#### Primary and secondary data collection

The research combined institutional records, environmental information, visitor data and complementary primary survey information.

Key activities included:

- Reviewing visitor registration records provided by Peru’s National Service of Natural Protected Areas (SERNANP).
- Collecting and organizing information on visitor numbers and visitor characteristics.
- Gathering biodiversity information, including the number of flora and fauna species, from institutional databases, conservation records and protected-area management plans.
- Collecting complementary information through visitor surveys.
- Compiling travel costs, distances, accessibility information and other relevant characteristics of the protected areas.
- Reviewing technical reports, academic literature and institutional publications to construct the analytical framework and research variables.

The visitor database contained 67,938 records from the six protected areas for 2016. From this database, a stratified random sample of 13,259 visitors was selected, according to the distribution of visits across protected areas and visitor categories (national and international). The study also used average visitor statistics from 2014–2016 to support the sampling design and subsequent economic valuation.

### 3. Methodology and analytical approach

The study applied an environmental economic valuation approach based on the Traditional Travel Cost Method (TTC), incorporating the Random Utility Model (RUM).

#### Random Utility Model – Conditional Logit

Applied a Conditional Logit Model to estimate visitors’ preferences when choosing between six alternative protected natural areas. The model incorporated several site-specific attributes, including:

- Number of flora species.
- Number of fauna species.
- Total travel cost.
- Total travel time.
- Accessibility and number of access routes.
- Tourism-designated areas.
- Other environmental and site characteristics considered in the research design.

Biodiversity was operationalized through species richness, measured by the number of recorded flora and fauna species in each protected area. The model allowed the research to examine how changes in environmental attributes and travel costs influenced the probability of selecting a particular destination.

#### Marginal effects analysis

Estimated marginal effects to measure how changes in biodiversity attributes and travel costs influenced the probability of choosing a protected natural area. This approach made it possible to assess the relative importance of biodiversity compared with the economic and accessibility-related factors influencing visitors’ decisions.

#### Economic valuation and welfare estimation

Applied the Random Utility Model framework to estimate the economic value visitors attributed to the characteristics of protected natural areas. The analysis provided monetary estimates of recreational use value, including the contribution of environmental attributes to visitors’ site preferences.

#### Statistical software

- Microsoft Excel: Data organization, sample selection and statistical processing.
- STATA14: Conditional Logit estimation, marginal effects and economic valuation calculations.

### 4. Key findings and results

#### Biodiversity significantly influences visitation decisions

The results confirmed that biodiversity, measured through the number of flora and fauna species, was a statistically significant attribute in visitors’ decisions to visit protected natural areas. Importantly, the study found that changes in biodiversity influenced visitation decisions as significantly as changes in travel costs. This challenged the assumption that travel expenses alone were sufficient to explain recreational demand for protected natural areas.

#### Differences between national and international visitors

The analysis identified differences in preferences between visitor groups.

- National visitors showed a positive preference for both flora and fauna species.
- International visitors showed a stronger preference for fauna species, while their estimated preference for flora was negative in the model.

These findings highlighted the importance of considering visitor segments when evaluating recreational preferences. They also reinforced the ecological interdependence of flora and fauna when designing conservation policies.

#### Other determinants of visitation

The research identified additional factors influencing site selection:

- Higher total travel costs reduced the probability of visiting a protected area.
- Longer travel times also reduced the probability of choosing a destination.
- The availability of access routes had a particularly strong influence on visitation decisions.
- Tourism-designated areas were statistically significant, although their influence was not decisive on their own.

### 5. Conclusions and recommendations

The research demonstrated that biodiversity is an important economic attribute of protected natural areas and that its influence on visitation decisions should be considered alongside travel costs and accessibility. The findings supported the use of environmental economic valuation as a tool for informing conservation and natural resource management decisions.

The research proposed several recommendations:

- **Integrate biodiversity into environmental policy and investment decisions:** Recognize the contribution of biodiversity to recreational value when designing conservation policies and allocating financial resources to protected areas.
- **Strengthen biodiversity conservation:** Ensure that environmental management strategies consider the interconnected roles of flora and fauna rather than focusing on individual species in isolation.
- **Promote sustainable tourism:** Encourage well-managed tourism as a potential source of economic benefits for protected areas and surrounding communities, while supporting conservation objectives.
- **Support local communities:** Strengthen the participation and economic opportunities of communities whose livelihoods depend on tourism and the natural resources provided by protected areas.
- **Improve future research:** Investigate potential substitution effects between flora and fauna attributes, which could not be identified under the model specifications used in this study.
- **Expand the scope of visitor research:** Include potential visitors who have not yet visited protected areas but may have an interest in doing so, complementing the evidence obtained from existing visitors.
