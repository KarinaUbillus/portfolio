## Research overview

This research investigated whether biodiversity influences people's decisions to visit protected natural areas in Peru and examined how environmental attributes contribute to recreational economic value.

Rather than estimating recreational demand solely through travel expenses, the study incorporated biodiversity—measured by recorded flora and fauna species—into an economic model of visitors' site-selection decisions.

## Research scope

The study covered six protected natural areas representing diverse ecological regions and tourism settings:

- Paracas National Reserve
- Manu National Park
- Huascarán National Park
- Pacaya Samiria National Reserve
- Nor Yauyos Cochas Landscape Reserve
- Tumbes Mangroves National Sanctuary

The visitor database contained 67,938 records for 2016. A stratified random sample of 13,259 national and international visitors was selected.

## Methodology

- Traditional Travel Cost Method
- Random Utility Model and Conditional Logit analysis
- Marginal effects analysis
- Economic welfare estimation
- Microsoft Excel and STATA 14

The model incorporated flora species, fauna species, travel cost, travel time, accessibility, access routes, tourism areas, and site characteristics.

## Key findings

Biodiversity significantly influenced visitation decisions. Changes in biodiversity affected site selection as strongly as changes in travel cost.

- National visitors showed positive preferences for flora and fauna.
- International visitors showed stronger preference for fauna, while flora preference was negative in the model.
- Higher travel costs and longer travel times reduced visitation probability.
- Access routes had a particularly strong influence on destination choice.

## Recommendations

- Integrate biodiversity into environmental policy and investment decisions
- Strengthen conservation strategies for interconnected flora and fauna
- Promote managed tourism that supports protected areas and local communities
- Improve future research on substitution effects and potential visitors
